# Decisions taken on the owner's behalf

You asked for the work to be finished without check-ins, so these decisions were made during the build.
Each one says what was decided, why, and what it costs if it turns out to be wrong.
Anything here can be reversed; tell us which and it will be changed.

## Product and safety rules

| # | Decision | Why | Cost if wrong |
|---|----------|-----|---------------|
| 1 | The employer-support label read from an advertisement ("stated in ad", "unavailable"…) is **informational only**. It is always shown with its quoted evidence and "verify with the employer". Automatic sending for a candidate who needs employer support is allowed only when staff recorded a **confirmed** label. | Reading free text for immigration-relevant claims proved impossible to make watertight: five review rounds each found new wrong positives. | Some genuine automatic applications wait for a human click. |
| 2 | **Acknowledgement evidence is staff-confirmed by default** (`INBOUND_AUTO_ACK_EVIDENCE=false`). A reply that looks like an acknowledgement opens a staff task; staff click to confirm. The automatic mode exists as an opt-in that accepts a small false-positive risk. | Same reason as 1. "Never show success without evidence" outranks convenience. | More manual clicks for staff. |
| 3 | `delivery_receipt` evidence can only come from the system (a signed provider report), never from a person typing it. Staff use `manual_confirmation` (note of ≥10 characters) or `employer_acknowledgement`. | A typed "receipt" proves nothing. | None. |
| 4 | Pausing the module, a source or a candidate **holds** queued applications (they go back to "approved" and are re-queued on resume). Withdrawing consent, suspending an account or deleting a candidate **cancels** them for good. | Pauses are operational; consent is a legal right. | None. |
| 5 | Automatic sending goes only to the employer's **verified contact address or an address at its verified domain**. A person-approved send may use an address named in the advertisement and the approver sees where the CV goes. | Advertisement text is untrusted. | One extra staff step per new employer. |
| 6 | Sensitive documents (passport, IDs, police certificate, medical, civil status…) are **deny-by-default**: only a short list of known-safe types is shared freely; everything else needs a candidate-granted, application-specific authorization with a stated need. | Free-text document names cannot be trusted. | Some ordinary documents ask for the candidate's authorization. |
| 7 | CVs that were never scanned for malware are **not attached** unless `ALLOW_UNSCANNED_ATTACHMENTS=true`. The start-up check refuses real mail with unscanned attachments and no ClamAV. | Safer default. | On a real server, applications stay in "preparing" until ClamAV is connected or the setting is deliberately changed. |
| 8 | Send caps default to **0** (no automatic sends) until an admin sets them. | Nothing sends by accident. | An admin must set caps before automatic mode does anything. |
| 9 | Only Admin/SuperAdmin can reassign or delete a candidate (a pre-existing route was changed). Staff can no longer take another staff member's candidate. | The old route defeated the data-isolation rule. | Staff who used to reassign candidates must ask an admin. |
| 10 | Only Admin/SuperAdmin can change an employer's verified domain/contact or rename it. | It decides where automatic sends go. | Managers must ask an admin. |
| 11 | Candidates see fixed reason codes in their timeline, never staff free text. | Privacy. | Less detail for candidates. |
| 12 | Staff login now has a rate limit and one generic error message; staff tokens carry a version (revocable) and last 12 h by default. | The login was open to the internet in the deployment kit. | A staff member can be locked for 15 minutes by someone guessing their email; a restart clears it. |
| 13 | The first-admin endpoint is closed to the internet (proxy rule + loopback-only + atomic). | Anyone could have created the first SuperAdmin on a fresh server. | `create-admin.sh` is the only way. |
| 14 | `trust-crm/.env` was removed from git tracking. | It held development credentials. | **Its values remain in git history; rotate anything used outside local development.** |
| 15 | An employer record carries province (the 13 Canadian codes), city, industry, phone, recruiter contact, up to 20 source URLs (http/https, no credentials), an eligibility note, a status (new, verified, contacted, replied, opted_out, blocked), a last-verified date and staff-only notes. Only Admin/SuperAdmin edit them (`PATCH /jobsearch/employers/:id`, same rule as #10); setting the status to `verified` stamps the verification date unless one is given; `notes` are shown only to the roles that can edit and the audit log records that notes changed, not their content. Statuses other than `opted_out` are set by staff; nothing moves an employer to `contacted` or `replied` automatically yet. | The record is what staff rely on before contacting a company; edits must be accountable. | Managers must ask an admin to correct a city or a note. |
| 16 | The contact history of an employer (`GET /jobsearch/employers/:id/history`, and a summary in the application-preparation response) lists applications, submissions and replies only for the caller's own candidates (admins: all). Contact by anyone else's candidates is a single number, "contacted by other candidates: N", with no names, ids, dates or jobs. | Avoid duplicate outreach without exposing another staff member's candidates. | An agent cannot see who else contacted the employer; they ask an admin. |
| 17 | The **do-not-contact list is absolute**. The pre-send guard refuses a send (and approval, queueing, automatic approval and follow-up replies refuse too) when the recipient address, its plus-address base, its domain or a parent domain is suppressed (`recipient_suppressed`), or when the employer is `opted_out` or `blocked` (`employer_opted_out`). Nothing overrides it: not human approval, not automatic mode, not a retry. Only an administrator can remove an entry, with a written reason, and the removal is audited. All staff can read and add entries (the list is not scoped by candidate). | A person who asked us to stop must not receive another e-mail because of a race, a retry or a click. | A wrong entry blocks a legitimate employer until an admin lifts it. |
| 18 | An inbound reply that asks to unsubscribe or not be contacted (English, French, a few Arabic phrases) is category `opt_out`, which outranks every other category and is **never** an acknowledgement. The patterns lean towards detecting (a false positive only adds a name to the list) but need an imperative or first-person form: the word "unsubscribe" in an automated footer is not a request. Only a reply matched with high confidence (reference or token, sender at the employer's verified domain, no DKIM/DMARC failure) and not an auto-reply or bounce acts: the sender address (plus-tag removed) is suppressed, the employer becomes `opted_out` (a `blocked` one stays blocked), one `opt_out_received` task opens for the responsible staff member, and it is audited. Nothing is cancelled or deleted, and an opt-out is never answered by a draft. Removing the employer's last opt-out suppression returns it to `replied`. | The legal and reputational cost of mailing someone who said stop is far above the cost of a manual undo. | An odd reply can wrongly silence one employer address until an admin undoes it. |
| 19 | A permanent delivery failure (a DSN with 5.x.x or `Action: failed`) for a sent application suppresses the address we sent to (source `bounce`); a soft bounce (4.x.x) never does, and the employer status is not changed. This includes 5.2.2 (mailbox full) and 5.7.x (our domain refused), which an admin may need to lift. | A dead address should not be retried; a retry harms the sending reputation. | Rare over-suppression after a provider-side policy block. |
| 20 | A domain suppression covers the domain and all its subdomains. There is no public-suffix list in the platform, so suppressing a shared suffix such as `co.uk` would cover everything under it; single-label names and free-mail providers are refused, and the suppression of one address never widens to its domain. | Keeps matching simple and fail-safe without bundled data. | Staff must not suppress a shared suffix by mistake. |
| 21 | Each application records its **outreach basis**. Only `published_vacancy_application` passes the guard in this stage; `employer_requested` and `other_documented` exist in the schema but are refused with `outreach_basis_not_permitted`, and no API changes the basis (it is a database value until a lawyer approves another basis). Outreach to a company that advertised nothing stays impossible. | CASL (Canada's anti-spam law) risk for unsolicited commercial e-mail. | A real "employer asked us to send" case needs a code change after a legal decision. |
| 22 | Every vacancy carries a **classification**: `verified_active`, `foreign_applicants_stated`, `eligibility_unclear`, `work_authorization_required`, `prospective_no_vacancy`, `closed_unsuitable`. It is derived in code when a job is stored, re-checked or relabelled, and is informational. Order: closed/expired/closing date passed, then work authorization required (the employer-support label is `unavailable`, or an allowlisted EN/FR phrase such as "must be legally entitled to work in Canada", "valid work permit required", "citoyens canadiens ou résidents permanents seulement" with no negation, hedge or offer of help in the sentence), then `foreign_applicants_stated`, then `verified_active`, otherwise `eligibility_unclear`. `foreign_applicants_stated` is **never inferred without a quoted evidence string**: the label must be `stated_in_ad` with a quote that occurs in the ad, or `confirmed` with its staff note; a conflict between a confirmation and the ad is only `eligibility_unclear`. The quote is stored (500 characters at most, enforced by a database CHECK) and is shown with the notice that it is **not proof of an LMIA, sponsorship or eligibility** (rule #1 is unchanged). `verified_active` needs an open vacancy with a real-looking ad (60+ characters, no talent-pool or "no current opening" wording) that a live, connected, non-manual source returned in the last 14 days; staff-entered vacancies are therefore `eligibility_unclear` until staff say otherwise. `prospective_no_vacancy` is set only by staff. | A vacancy label must not turn an advertisement into an immigration promise, and "open" is not the same as "verified". | Some genuine vacancies stay `eligibility_unclear` until a person classifies them. |
| 23 | Staff may override a classification (`POST /jobsearch/jobs/:id/classification`, `jobsearch:write`, audited with the previous value). `foreign_applicants_stated` needs an evidence quote that occurs **verbatim** in the stored description (case-insensitive, white space normalised, 8 characters at least, 500 at most); `prospective_no_vacancy` and `closed_unsuitable` need a note (kept in the audit trail); evidence, when given for any class, must also be a verbatim quote. A staff classification is **sticky**: no new sighting, re-check or confirmation changes it (`classified_by` stays set) until staff send `classification: null`, which hands the job back to the automatic rules. | The automatic rules are conservative; a person who read the ad can know better, but cannot invent a quote. | A closed job keeps a staff label until someone clears it (the job status, which blocks preparation, still follows the sources). |
| 24 | **Match bands** are stored on every match: 85 and over `strong`, 70-84 `good`, 50-69 `manual_review`, under 50 `not_recommended`. **Essential vetoes are hard overrides that can only lower a band**: outside the preferred destinations or a closed vacancy force `not_recommended`; the candidate needing employer support while the ad says it is unavailable, an ad that says applicants must already be in the country (both stay reviewable matches, as before: a person decides), a missing required language or licence, a confirmation that contradicts the ad, or a work-authorization-required ad for a candidate who needs support caps the band at `manual_review`. A vetoed match is never `strong` or `good`. Candidates see the band label, never the staff decision or note. `MATCH_MIN_SCORE` (default 40, which matches are stored) and `MATCH_AUTO_PREPARE_MIN` (default 60) are unchanged; stored matches in `not_recommended` stay visible to staff but are **never auto-proposed**: the scheduler skips them (and the system path of `createFromMatch` refuses them with `match_not_recommended`), and so it skips matches staff rejected. A person can still open an application on a flagged match. | The score alone must not outvote a hard fact about the job. | A few good-looking matches wait for a person because of a veto. |
| 25 | Staff **accept or reject a match** (`POST /jobsearch/matches/:id/accept|reject`, `jobsearch:approve`: Manager/Admin; an Agent gets 403; outside the caller's candidate scope is 404). Repeating the same decision changes nothing and writes no audit row; a different decision, or a different note, is an audited change that records the previous value. The note (500 characters) is staff-only. Decisions survive re-matching and protect the match from being pruned. Candidates see no decision in their match list; once an application exists they see only `pending/accepted/rejected`. | Approval at the match level lets a manager screen before any application is prepared. | None. |
| 26 | The module setting **`require_match_acceptance`** (default **false**, so nothing changes until an administrator turns it on: `PUT /jobsearch/match-settings`, admin only, audited) makes application preparation refuse a match that is not accepted (409 `match_not_accepted`), including a match that no longer exists; the scheduler then only creates applications for accepted matches. Preparation is also refused for classification `closed_unsuitable` (409 `job_closed_unsuitable`) and for `work_authorization_required` unless the candidate's recorded `work_authorization_status` is `work_permit_open`, `work_permit_employer_specific`, `pr` or `citizen` (409 `work_authorization_required`); when allowed, the package carries the note `job_requires_work_authorization:candidate_status_<status>`. A recorded status is staff-entered; it is not verified by the platform. | A permit is a fact about the candidate; an ad cannot grant it. | A candidate whose permit is not recorded waits for staff to record it. |
| 27 | Application preparation is also refused for classification `prospective_no_vacancy` (409 `no_vacancy_outreach_not_permitted`), for every candidate status. Prospective outreach stays blocked until a lawyer approves its CASL basis (see #21). | A talent-pool page or a "no current opening" posting is outreach without a vacancy. | A genuine employer-invited approach needs staff to change the classification (with a note) after the legal decision. |

## Engineering choices

- Extra migrations 003–010 were added where a table or column was really needed (all additive, idempotent, verified on existing data by `scripts/verify-migrations.js`).
- Search retries, replies and sends run through a database-backed queue with advisory locks; locks are namespaced by schema so tests cannot interfere.
- The app connects to Postgres as an unprivileged role in the deployment kit.
- Restore replaces the database only after the restored copy and the uploads archive validated.
- A few small fixes (a one-line pass-through for rate-limit back-off, the seed-script guards, the third deployment re-review) were accepted without a further independent review round because their tests fail on the old code; the final whole-branch review and the later targeted reviews cover them.

## Stage 8 (Canada service layer): decisions 28-33

| # | Decision | Why | Cost if wrong |
|---|----------|-----|---------------|
| 28 | Every candidate gets a **reference `CAN-YYYY-NNNN`** at creation (year of creation, a gap-free per-year counter under a lock; existing candidates were backfilled in id order). It is immutable and unique. | One stable id for files, reports and phone calls. | A reference issued by mistake is never reused or changed; the number is simply skipped from use (the counter only moves forward). |
| 29 | A **Canadian-format CV** is built only from the candidate's **confirmed** profile (the profile the candidate reviewed and confirmed), city/country and the staff-set candidate record. No photo, date of birth, age, nationality, marital status, passport or salary line; list items that look like such data are dropped and flagged. The summary uses a fixed template and only appears when profession, whole years and a skill are all confirmed. Files are PDF and DOCX, named `<reference>-CV-V<n>[-FR]`, immutable once stored. A new version is a new row. A version can be attached to an application only when **staff approved it (Manager/Admin) and the candidate authorised it**, and only while the profile still matches the facts it was built from. No machine translation: French mode only changes headings and the summary template. | Invented or sensitive content in a CV is the worst failure; a generated file is still a document the candidate signs off. | A candidate who wants a date of birth in the CV needs a code change; an edited profile invalidates old versions until a new one is approved. |
| 30 | **Reports** (company PDF/XLSX, candidate "Canada Job Search Activity Report" PDF/XLSX) use the same numbers as the JSON report; the verified list contains **only submissions the provider accepted** (`sent`, `confirmed_submitted`). Every generation stores an insert-only snapshot (payload hash and files). Candidates download only their own; staff inside their scope. Dates follow the report timezone (`REPORT_TIMEZONE`, else `HR_TIMEZONE`, else Africa/Tunis). Scheduled company snapshots are **off** until an Administrator or `REPORT_SCHEDULE_ENABLED` turns them on. Every report carries the disclaimer that it promises no job, LMIA, permit or visa. | Reports are what staff show candidates; they must be checkable against raw rows. | A snapshot of a candidate who is later erased is not deleted automatically (see the checklist). |
| 31 | **Candidate fees are gated.** A service of kind `candidate_fee` (Administrator only) can be put on an invoice or paid (new payments) only when the candidate's jurisdiction (`CA-` plus the desired province, else `FIN_DEFAULT_JURISDICTION`) has a latest **legal review** with status `approved` and not expired, **and** the candidate has an **active signed contract** for that service. A `prohibited` review blocks invoices and new payments (refunds and voids stay possible). Reviews are insert-only; the newest decides. The platform **records** a review made by the owner's lawyer; it never decides legality and gives no legal advice. The old invoice route refuses candidates with an active fee contract (`candidate_fee_use_catalogue`). | The legal position on candidate fees depends on the province and the licence; that opinion is the owner's. | A candidate in a province with no recorded review cannot be charged until an Administrator records one. |
| 32 | Application preparation attaches the **latest eligible Canadian CV** (PDF preferred, DOCX if only that exists, French for a French job when one is eligible) and records the exact version, file and hash in the package; otherwise the approved uploaded CV is used with a note. While an eligible Canadian CV exists, an explicit request for the uploaded original is refused. Withdrawing the candidate authorisation blocks use immediately and cancels queued applications using that version. | The CV the staff approved and the candidate authorised must be the CV the employer receives. | Staff cannot send the uploaded original while an eligible Canadian CV exists unless they withdraw it. |
| 33 | All stage-8 staff routes sit behind the same scope rules as the rest of the module (Admin all, Manager/Receptionist/Agent assigned candidates, HR/Accountant none for candidate data); Employer, Agency and Candidate tokens are refused, and the all-routes sweep classifies every new route. | No new route may widen who can see a candidate. | None. |

## Not done on purpose
- No real job source (Job Bank, LinkedIn, Indeed), mailbox, ClamAV, WhatsApp/Meta channel or external calendar sync is connected. No scraping.
- No Gmail OAuth/API adapter: it needs the owner's Google Cloud credentials. The SMTP/IMAP adapter and the signed inbound webhook remain.
- No LLM: CVs, summaries and letters come from deterministic templates; nothing is machine-translated.
- No NOC/TEER mapping: it needs the official reference data from the owner.
- Prospective outreach (to an employer with no advertised vacancy) is blocked by design until a lawyer approves a CASL basis (#21, #27).
- No legal opinion is given or decided by the platform (candidate fees, recruiter licensing, immigration advice): the owner's lawyer decides; the platform only records (#31).
- No tokenised candidate link: candidates sign in with an e-mail code.
- No payment gateway or bank connection (payments are recorded by the Accountant); no payroll.
- No data migration from the old EMNA CRM.
- No automatic erasure path for generated CVs and report snapshots of an erased candidate (see docs/vita-canada/CHECKLIST.md).

The rest of the original platform brief **now exists** as separate modules, each with its own setup and checklist: the public website and CRM core (`docs/vita-website-crm`), the employer and agency portals (`docs/vita-employer-agency`), accounting (`docs/vita-accounting`), attendance and HR (`docs/vita-hr`), messaging (`docs/vita-messaging`) and the read-only MCP assistant endpoint (`docs/vita-mcp`). None of them has been deployed to a real server (see their checklists).
